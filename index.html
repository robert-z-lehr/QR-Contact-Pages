2. QR settings

What do M, Q, and H mean?

M (~15%) is the recommended default and usually gives the best balance between QR density and damage tolerance. Q (~25%) adds more redundancy for scratches, printing defects, or wear, but makes the QR denser. H (~30%) provides the highest standard error correction, but usually produces the smallest modules at the same physical size.

Higher error correction does not automatically make a QR easier to scan. At a fixed physical size, more redundancy can make each module smaller.

Making a reliable QR

What this QR does

The generated QR stores vCard contact information directly. A compatible phone camera or QR scanner can interpret the contact data without retrieving a hosted contact file.

Less data usually means a simpler QR

Longer names, titles, websites, and descriptions increase the payload. More payload can increase the QR version and matrix size, which makes modules smaller at a fixed physical size.

Physical size matters

Pixel resolution and physical dimensions are different. A high-resolution PNG can still be difficult to scan if it is printed or engraved too small. The module-width guidance on this page is a practical production heuristic, not a QR standard requirement.

Keep the border clear

The square download includes a four-module white quiet zone around the QR. Do not place text, graphics, borders, or engraving inside that clear area. Circular styling is decorative and should be tested more carefully because a circular boundary is not the conventional square quiet-zone geometry.

Test before producing multiples

Test the downloaded QR at its final physical size, on the intended material, under varied lighting, and with multiple phones before printing, engraving, or fabricating a batch.

Direct QR limitation

The contact information is fixed inside the QR. If a phone number, email address, or other encoded detail changes, generate a new QR.
